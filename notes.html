The most interesting person is you.

Because if you think about it, when you think that somebody else is interesting it’s subjective. Interesting is personal. Interesting is a judgment based on what you think, based on you, what’s internal to you.

I think it is possible to create and show it on social media and not have your joy of creating taken away by it

I feel like people, like me, create and go publish their creations on social media, with good intention, with good hearts, with hope and initial joy.

Then the noise comes, all the other people, all the buttons, the options, the styles, the advice, the decisions, the bios, confuses our spark and makes us wonder how stupid we were to even try putting ourselves in the middle of all this.

So we go back and re-search and search and search, trying to find the version that when we get the courage to get back there again, this time it will be perfect.

But social media is not designed to be this way. It’s designed to have us keep coming back for answers and inspirations, that never quite feels whole or complete.

That said, I don’t think social media is evil, “head off with social media”, actually no. I think it’s great.

It’s a great place to get your creations marketed with super specialised algorithms to reach ‘our people’. But I do also think we should know how it works, why it makes us feel the way it does and use it in the way that serves us, and not let it squish our beauty and creativity by not knowing how it affects us.

Create, get in, get out, go create again and listen only to yourself, put on your noise cancelling headphones from everything else, you don’t need it, you already have everything you need and we all want to know what it is.

“Is this good?” is an unanswerable question

Instead of asking if what you’ve created is ‘good’, which is not even answerable because you’re likely asking whether other people think it’s good and you cannot know what other people think, and even if you could ‘good’ is subjective and different to everyone.

Instead ask yourself if what you have created is expressing what you were trying to express with it. This, you’ll always be able to find an answer to, either by looking deeper inside you to find more details about what you’re trying to express, or improving your skill (writing, videos, cooking, etc) at expressing it. This one you have full control over, this one does not make you go in infinite doubt loops. This one has a satisfying, answerable answer.

You can’t burn out from being you

I think burnout only happens when we don’t listen to our core selves and do what we are genuinely curious about doing. So I think when people say that they’re burned out–and people even get medical level burnouts where they end up in hospital and need medication and weeks or months of rest–I think it’s because they were forcing themselves to do what they don’t want to do.

And that’s quite serious.

It’s like literally forcing yourself to spend your limited time living life you don’t want to live. Maybe it’s even a good thing that your body forces you to stop, maybe it’s hoping it can make you think. And I think it works because people do start thinking about the meaning of life at this point.

It actually happened to Vanessa Lau. She got burned out, it forced her to think about what she really wanted, what was really core to her, and now she’s creating like fire and watching her is inspiring. It feels like if she keeps going that way, there’s no end to what she could do.

That’s what I feel now too. The more I listen to myself, the more ideas I have. They just keep coming. I feel so much hope.

So, with this logic, I don’t think it’s possible to burn out doing something you’re genuinely curious about. To feel tired, yes, but not burned out. And in a way I’m thankful that you can burn out from not being you. It’s like a fail safe mechanism in a machine, when something goes wrong and it’s dangerous, it turns everything off and you need to find the issue and fix it.

Rules are comforting

Rules create a path that we think if we follow, we’ll reach happiness. So we either follow the rules someone else has created (12 Rules for Life, influencers, religions, the lives of others) or we create our own.

Rules give the comfort of hope that there is meaning to life to be found. I believe there is no meaning, and that’s uncomfortable to think about. It was for me, and I think it is for the people who “follow”.

I think we create our own paths, and just living life on the path we create is the only meaning to life. And if we want to make money from living a meaningful life, we share our “rules”, which are just words we use to describe the way we already lived.

I really don’t think that following someone else’s rules is the best way to live, but I also believe it helps many, just like rules of religions help many too.

The most modern type of “priest” with a “religion” is an influencer. They spread a belief, they have followers, they need budget for marketing to spread the belief and gain more followers. People pay them to get the details of how to live their path. More money, more message spread, more followers.

You can either choose a church, which seems to be helpful for a lot of people, or you can create your own path.

I think it’s good to know we have options.

It gives us reassurance that if so many other people also believe that they found the right path, that path must be a good one to follow. One that will give meaning to our life and make us happy too.

We follow the paths of others, hoping they found the “right one”

I feel like there is no meaning to life, like a “right path” or “the right thing to do”. But before we realise that, we think that there is. That’s why social media is so addictive. People show their lives and that makes us feel that they have figured it out.

That’s why we “follow” them. We literally feel like we want to follow their path and get the life that they’re showing us they’re having. If we use the products that they use, travel to the places they have, create the type of business that they have, maybe we can follow that and that would be a nice path for us.

I think every time we look at the lives of others we hope that their path could be one that we could follow too, since we think there is a right and wrong path, like one that we create would not make us happy.

But it never does. We never manage to follow others exactly. It never works. Something feels missing. And we either realise that there aren’t right paths that have been figured out, and create our own, or we keep searching, keep hoarding information on “the right things to do for a nice life”, but it’s an impossible task.

Trying to drink the ocean is even more realistic.
